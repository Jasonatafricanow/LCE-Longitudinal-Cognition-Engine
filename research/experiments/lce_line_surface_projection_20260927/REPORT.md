# LCE Line -> Surface Projection Experiment\n\nDate: 2026-09-27\nBranch: verification/lce-line-surface-projection-20260927\nBase: callable-projection branch f4b97481a1920ab1ef9fceaf977c15849de4faf4\n\n## Question\n\nCan multiple mature logic lines form a higher-order Surface because their change trajectories are structurally similar even when their raw semantics are far apart?\n\nA second requirement is engineering usefulness: the Surface must not become an attraction black hole for concrete dialogue. Local queries should still retrieve their local Line projection; a Surface should become callable when the relation itself is the useful object.\n\n## Fixture design\n\nThree primary lines are placed in nearly orthogonal semantic regions:\n\n- trading\n- engineering\n- writing\n\nEach line has four states. Their domain anchors differ strongly, so raw points across domains are almost unrelated. Their local displacement sequences are identical.\n\nA fourth distractor line has a different trajectory shape.\n\nThe operator compares translation-invariant sequences of local displacements. It does not compare the raw domain centroids.\n\nNo abstraction level field or semantic taxonomy is assigned.\n\n## Result 1 — raw semantics are far apart\n\nAcross the three primary lines:\n\n    maximum cross-line raw cosine = 0.0355868\n    mean cross-line raw cosine    = 0.0074687\n\nSo the higher-order match is not coming from ordinary raw semantic proximity.\n\n## Result 2 — the three lines share one trajectory shape\n\nPairwise trajectory-shape similarity:\n\n    trading <-> engineering = 1.0\n    trading <-> writing     = 1.0\n    engineering <-> writing = 1.0\n\nThe distractor line had shape similarity 0.748579 to each primary line.\n\nWith the experiment threshold at 0.95, exactly one Surface candidate was emitted:\n\n    {line_trading, line_engineering, line_writing}\n\nThe distractor was not included.\n\nThis is an operator-capability result only. The 0.95 threshold and synthetic vector geometry are not proposed as production policy.\n\n## Result 3 — Surface support still closes to Raw Evidence\n\nThe Surface is implemented as a derived Projection whose sources are the three Line projections.\n\nIts provenance closure expands through those Lines to exactly 12 Raw Evidence IDs:\n\n    trade_0..trade_3\n    eng_0..eng_3\n    write_0..write_3\n\nNo Line or Surface node becomes independent evidence.\n\nTherefore:\n\n    Raw -> Line -> Surface\n\nremains a compiled dependency graph rather than a self-supporting evidence graph.\n\n## Result 4 — local dialogue still retrieves the local Line\n\nConcrete/local queries were evaluated over the same callable projection index that also contained the Surface.\n\nResults:\n\n    trading query     -> line_trading\n    engineering query -> line_engineering\n    writing query     -> line_writing\n\nFor each local query, Surface score was 0.0.\n\nThis is the desired engineering boundary: the mere existence of a high-order Surface does not force it into every dialogue that touches one member domain.\n\n## Result 5 — relation-level query retrieves the Surface\n\nThe fixture also exposes a callable representation of the shared trajectory relation itself.\n\nFor that relation-level query:\n\n    Surface score       = 1.0\n    each member Line    = 0.5\n    distractor Line     = 0.125\n\nand Surface ranked first.\n\nThis demonstrates a useful separation without any explicit Level-1/Level-2/Level-3 gate:\n\n    local semantic request -> local Line\n    shared trajectory relation -> Surface\n\nThe distinction emerges from the projection contents and dependency structure.\n\nImportant limitation: the relation query is represented using synthetic shape tokens generated by the experiment. This does not yet prove that natural-language semantic compilation can produce an equally good callable Surface representation.\n\n## Result 6 — a corrected member Line can dissolve the Surface\n\nThe trading line was recompiled with a materially different trajectory shape.\n\nBefore correction:\n\n    Surface count = 1\n\nAfter correction:\n\n    Surface count = 0\n    current status = UNKNOWN\n\nThe historical Surface still retained its full 12-item provenance closure for audit, while invalidating the four trading Raw items reduced current-valid Raw closure to 8.\n\nSo a higher-order abstraction is not permanent merely because it once existed.\n\n    historical provenance remains inspectable\n    current qualifying support can shrink\n    Surface can return to UNKNOWN\n\nThis preserves the same falsifiability rule already established for Line projections.\n\n## Verification\n\nImplementation/workflow head before this report: ad7bf0579ea5d9d04ca42d24ec4f998e66b68a0e\n\n- LCE Line Surface Projection run 36321505157: PASS\n- focused invariants: 8 passed\n- Public Verification is run on every verification-branch commit; The remaining structural risks are:\n\n1. Different-length trajectories: the same higher-order change expressed through 3, 7, or 20 states.\n2. Branching Worktrees: only one branch of a Line participates in a Surface while sibling branches do not.\n3. Partial overlap: one Line legitimately participates in more than one Surface.\n4. Irregular dual-time history: retroactively inserted states change a Line and therefore may create or dissolve a Surface.\n5. Real callable projection: replace synthetic shape tokens with an actual semantic projection of the derived relation and test consumer precision.\n
